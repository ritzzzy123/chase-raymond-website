7 Factors To Consider

When buying a home

Purchasing a home today is a far more complicated process than it used to be. Here are the seven factors worth investigating before you commit.

1

Lifestyle

The number one factor: finding you a home that suits your family's needs and budget.

2

Your investment & equity building

Your new home should also be a good investment, one that realizes a solid return over your time owning it. Some buyers prioritize lifestyle fit over equity, and that's valid too, but it's worth weighing both.

3

Mortgage financing

Very few buyers pay cash. It's not just about interest rate, prepayment privileges, doubling up on payments, and bi-weekly options can all change how fast you build equity.

4

Site influences

Future development, road systems, and other site factors that could affect your home's future value.

5

Reputable builder

For new builds, confirming a reputable builder protects your future resale value.

6

Inspections

Knowing what inspections a property needs, home, septic, well, electrical, roof, helps avoid costly surprises after closing.

7

Hidden savings

Mortgage payments and rent are often similar, but a portion of every mortgage payment reduces your principal. That's a built-in form of savings renting doesn't offer.

Step 1

Mortgage Pre-Approval

Mortgage broker

Not limited to one lender, brokers have access to the whole market and can find terms, conditions, and rates that best suit your needs.

Financial institution

Options are more limited since they only offer their own products, but a strong banking relationship can still make a real difference.

Tips

  • Establish what you can afford before house hunting starts, both the down payment and what you can carry monthly.
  • Hidden costs beyond the down payment: inspections, appraisal fees, mortgage insurance, CMHC fees, legal fees, land transfer tax, title insurance.
  • Stay within your price range. Most REALTORs won't show homes unless you're pre-approved, and it keeps comparisons apples to apples.

Documents needed for your mortgage approval

Notice of Assessment from previous taxation year(s), Letter of Employment, T4s, Proof of Down Payment. Your mortgage broker will confirm anything else needed.

Pre-Qualification vs. Pre-Approval

How much home can you afford?

Pre-qualification defines the numbers. Pre-approval shows you the money.

Pre-qualification

Establishes how much you can afford to borrow based on income and debt. It's not an assurance of approval.

Pre-approval

A detailed approach that gives you a firm decision on a home loan, and makes you a "cash buyer" in the seller's eyes, with real negotiating and bargaining power in a multiple-offer situation.

Step 2

Costs of Buying a Home

Down payment requirements

  • Purchase price up to $500,000: minimum 5% down
  • $500,000 to $1 million: 5% of the first $500,000 plus 10% of the remaining balance
  • $1 million or more: minimum 20% down

Conventional vs. high ratio

Conventional: down payment is 20% or more of the purchase price.
High ratio: down payment is less than 20%, and must be insured through CMHC, Sagen, or Canada Guaranty. Premiums typically range 0.6% to 4.5% of the mortgage, paid up front or added to the loan.

Other costs to plan for

  • Appraisal & application fee: ~$250 to $350
  • RRSP monies: up to $35,000 per individual for first-time buyers
  • Building inspections: ~$500 to $600, highly recommended
  • Buyer rebate: first-time buyer land transfer tax rebate up to $4,000
  • Legal fees & disbursements: ~$1,200 to $1,500 (title search, status certificate for condos, registering mortgage/deed, office disbursements)
  • Survey: ~$2,000 to $5,000 if one doesn't exist, title insurance covers the gap
  • Title insurance: ~$400, a must on all real property purchases

Land transfer tax

Paid on closing to the Treasurer of Ontario, calculated on final purchase price:

  • 0.5% up to $55,000
  • 1% from $55,001 to $250,000
  • 1.5% from $250,001 to $400,000
  • 2% from $400,001 to $2,000,000
  • 2.5% above $2,000,001

*First-time buyers may qualify for a rebate up to $4,000.

All fees, costs, and disbursements listed are approximate. Actual figures should be confirmed directly with your lawyer.

Step 6

Preparing an Offer

Every offer gets structured around your specific needs and the property you're buying. Here's what typically goes into one.

Price

Set an initial offer that avoids offending the seller, using the CMA as our foundation.

Deposit

Varies by price and competition, often $10,000 minimum or more. Some sellers accept a lower initial deposit with more due upon condition removal.

Conditions

Financing, inspections, insurance, site influences, lawyer approval, sale of your current home, whatever protects you.

Chattels included

Usually outlined in the MLS® listing, we'll confirm anything you want added or anything the seller included that you don't.

Terms

Balance due on closing, chattels in good working order, right to a final walkthrough, survey requests, tenancy info, and more.

Rental equipment & exclusions

I'll help confirm costs on any rented equipment and potential buyout options.

Irrevocable

The window one party gives the other to accept an offer before it becomes null and void.

Closing date

When you're required to pay in full and take possession, plan your move accordingly.

Step 7

Negotiating the Offer

The seller's goal is the highest price on their terms, yours is the lowest price on your terms. Market conditions play a huge factor in the advice I'll give you here.

Offer foundation

We look at what's sold recently and what's currently available so you have clarity on where to start your bid and where you're willing to end up.

You're at a disadvantage as a buyer

The seller owns the property and can accept or reject any offer, and can even raise their asking price during negotiation.

Appealing to the seller

We structure limited conditions to appeal to the seller, without ever compromising your protection. Giving the seller the closing date they want can matter more than price.

Multiple offers

Staying reachable matters, sellers may counter the most responsive buyer first. You may not get a second chance to improve your offer.

When an offer is presented, five things can happenThe seller accepts it, rejects it, rejects it with a counter, refuses to do anything, or takes the property off the market entirely.
Step 8

Satisfying the Conditions

Buyers typically get up to five business days (excluding weekends and statutory holidays) to remove conditions and proceed.

Financing

Your lender needs the Agreement of Purchase and Sale, a feature sheet with front exterior photo, and your supporting documents.

Inspection

A qualified inspector checks for deficiencies or major structural issues, could include septic or environmental inspections too.

Insurance

Confirms with your provider that the home can actually be insured.

Lawyer approval

Optional but recommended, your lawyer reviews the Agreement of Purchase and Sale for legal opinion and approval.

Home inspections

A home inspection is a visual examination of the home's condition at the time of inspection. It covers roofing, chimneys and flashing, exterior, drainage, structure, electrical, heating and cooling, insulation, plumbing, foundation, and interior, usually about three hours.

A quality inspection uncovers major defects (typically $1,000+ repairs) before they become expensive surprises, and gives you peace of mind about what you're actually buying. It's highly recommended you attend in person.

Cost

Typically $500 to $600 plus HST, a small price against the cost of a major deficiency discovered after you own the home. Make sure your inspector gives a written report, a verbal one won't help you months later.

Choosing an inspector

Look for a Registered Home Inspector (RHI) through the Ontario Association of Home Inspectors (OAHI), members follow a strict code of ethics and standards of practice.

Step 9

Fulfilling the Conditions

Once you're satisfied with every condition, they get removed by way of a Notice of Fulfillment, a Waiver, or an Amendment. You sign it and it's delivered to the seller before the conditional period expires. Once they acknowledge receipt, you've bought your new home. (If removing conditions by Amendment, the seller has to agree to it, not just acknowledge it.)

I handle sending all required documentation to your lawyer, who takes it from there through to closing. I'll stay in touch with you the whole way, before and after closing, for anything that comes up.

Glossary

Terms worth knowing

Agreement of Purchase & Sale

A written document where the buyer agrees to buy and the seller agrees to sell under stated terms and conditions.

Amortization

The length of time it takes to pay off a mortgage in full through equal installments of principal and interest.

Closing statement

The financial disclosure accounting for all funds received and expected at closing, including deposits and mortgage life insurance.

Conditions

A requirement that must be satisfied before a contract becomes binding, for example financing approval.

Condominium

Ownership of a specific unit plus a proportionate interest in common areas.

Cooperative

A corporation or trust holds title to the property and grants occupancy rights to shareholders.

CMHC

Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation, provides default insurance on mortgages under 20% down.

Deposit

Financial security given by the buyer as a commitment to complete the transaction, credited toward the purchase price at closing.

Gross Debt Service Ratio (GDS)

The percentage of gross income that can go toward housing costs, including mortgage payments, taxes, and condo fees where applicable.

Home inspection report

A qualified inspector's report on a property's overall condition, structure and mechanical systems included.

Home warranty plan

Protection against failure of mechanical systems, may include plumbing, electrical, heating, and appliances.

Joint tenancy

Equal undivided ownership by two or more people, survivors typically take the deceased's interest.

Lien

A legal hold or claim on property as security for a debt.

Loan-to-value ratio

The mortgage amount relative to the appraised value, expressed as a percentage.

Mortgage commitment

A written promise to provide a loan for a specified amount on specified terms.

P.I.T.

Principal, interest, and taxes.

REALTOR®

A real estate broker or salesperson who is an active member of CREA and the local real estate board, and follows a strict code of ethics.

One More Thing

A few tips for buyers

  • Choose a REALTOR® you're comfortable with, someone honest and trustworthy who actually understands your needs.
  • Decide your needs and wants before you start looking, and lean on your REALTOR®'s advice.
  • Get pre-approved, know how much you can actually afford.
  • Stick to your budget, you still want to go out for dinner and a movie sometime.
  • Get your mortgage documentation ready ahead of time, T4s, employment letter, proof of down payment.
  • Know how much the home is worth so you don't pay too much.
  • Avoid getting into a bidding war, you might pay more than you should.
  • Don't get overwhelmed by the current owner's decor, picture your own furniture there instead.
  • Look at the bones of the home, even one that needs a coat of paint might still suit you.
  • Location, location, location.
  • Think about proximity to work, school, and transit.
  • Get the home professionally inspected.
  • If you're renting, you might already qualify to buy without realizing it, talk to a mortgage rep.
  • Get a lawyer, usually a referral from family, friends, or your REALTOR®.
