7 Factors To Consider

When selling a home

Selling a home well takes more than putting a sign in the yard. Here are the seven factors worth getting right before your home hits the market.

1

Timing

London's market moves in seasonal patterns. Listing at the right moment, or waiting a few weeks, can change your outcome more than almost anything else.

2

Pricing strategy

Overpricing costs you more than underpricing. Homes that sit too long start to look like something's wrong, even when nothing is.

3

Presentation & staging

First impressions happen online, in photos, before a buyer ever steps inside. Small, targeted work here outperforms expensive renovations.

4

Marketing reach

Your listing needs to actually get seen, MLS®, professional photography, social media, and my personal network all play a role.

5

Showings & access

The easier your home is to show, the more offers you're likely to see. Flexibility here is leverage.

6

Closing flexibility

A closing date that works for the buyer can be worth more to them than a lower price, that's real negotiating room for you.

7

Net proceeds planning

Knowing what you'll actually walk away with, after commission, legal fees, and mortgage payout, before you list, not after.

Step 1

Listing Consultation

Choosing the right REALTOR®

Choose someone who gives you real advice on pricing, timing, and marketing, not just what you want to hear. Your agent should have connections with lawyers, home inspectors, stagers, and contractors, and a real strategy for getting your home in front of serious buyers.

What happens at the consultation

1

I walk the property in person and get a feel for its condition, layout, and standout features.

2

We talk through your goals, timeline, and what "sold" needs to look like for you.

3

I walk you through comparable sales data so pricing decisions are grounded in real numbers, not guesses.

TipsBe upfront about your timeline, it changes the strategy. Ask questions, a good agent wants you informed, not just along for the ride.
Step 2

Home Evaluation & Pricing

Make sure you actually know what your home is worth before you set a price. I'll prepare a Comprehensive Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) so the number we land on is grounded in today's market, not last year's.

1

Similar homes recently sold

Tells us what buyers are actually paying for homes like yours, in your area, right now.

2

Similar homes currently for sale

Tells us what you're competing against, and how buyers will judge your home against the alternatives.

3

Similar homes that didn't sell

Expired or terminated listings usually flag pricing problems worth avoiding.

TipsPrice it right from day one, the first two weeks on market get the most attention. Don't chase the market down with repeated price drops, it signals desperation to buyers.
Step 3

Prep & Staging

Small, targeted work almost always outperforms big renovations dollar for dollar. Here's where to focus.

Worth doing

  • Declutter and depersonalize every room
  • Deep clean, including carpets and windows
  • Fresh, neutral paint where it needs it
  • Fix the small stuff, leaky taps, loose handles, burnt-out bulbs
  • Curb appeal, lawn, entryway, front door
  • Professional staging or a consult, even light staging moves the needle

Usually skip

  • Full kitchen or bathroom renovations right before listing
  • Major structural changes with no clear ROI
  • Trendy finishes that narrow your buyer pool
  • Anything that won't be done and cleaned up before photos
TipsAsk me before spending on any renovation, not every upgrade pays for itself at resale. A storage unit for excess furniture is often the best money you'll spend.
Step 4

Photos & Marketing

Most buyers decide whether to book a showing based on photos alone, this is the highest-leverage part of your listing.

What goes into it

  • Professional photography, every listing
  • Video or a virtual tour for the right property
  • A feature sheet highlighting what makes your home stand out
  • Yard sign and MLS® syndication to REALTOR.ca and beyond

Where it's promoted

  • MLS® and REALTOR.ca
  • Social media, targeted and organic
  • My buyer network and other agents at my brokerage
  • Email to my active buyer contacts
Step 5

Going Live

Your listing goes live on MLS® and everywhere else simultaneously. The first seven days on market typically get the most traffic and the most serious interest, so everything above needs to be locked in before launch day, not scrambled together after.

TipsHave the home fully show-ready before we go live, the first showings often matter most. Be ready for a wave of activity in week one, that's normal and it's a good sign.
Step 6

Showings & Open Houses

The easier your home is to access, the more buyers get through the door, and more buyers generally means more offers.

What to expect

Showings are booked through a lockbox/showing service with as much notice as possible. I collect feedback after every showing and pass it along so we can adjust if needed.

Open houses

Used strategically, not as a default, some homes benefit, others sell faster through private showings alone. I'll tell you honestly which approach fits your property.

TipsBe flexible with showing times, turning down requests costs you buyers. Leave for showings when possible, buyers speak more freely and stay longer without the seller present.
Step 7

Offers & Negotiation

Your goal is the highest price under your terms, the buyer's is the lowest price under theirs. I handle the back-and-forth so you get the strongest terms available.

What we review together

  • Price, how it compares to your CMA and current market activity
  • Deposit, the amount and timing offered
  • Conditions, financing, inspection, insurance, sale of the buyer's home
  • Chattels & fixtures, what's included or excluded

Also worth weighing

  • Closing date, does it work with your own move
  • Irrevocable, how long you have to respond
  • Buyer strength, pre-approved vs. conditional on financing
Multiple offersIf more than one buyer is interested, I'll walk you through strategy, whether that's setting an offer date, reviewing all offers together, or negotiating with one buyer directly. Every situation is different, and I'll tell you honestly what gives you the best outcome.
Step 8

Conditions & Due Diligence

Once you've accepted an offer, the buyer typically has a window (often up to five business days) to satisfy their conditions before the sale becomes firm.

What's usually expected of you

  • Reasonable access for the buyer's home inspection
  • Providing documents your lawyer or lender requests
  • Staying flexible on scheduling during this window

If issues come up

If the buyer's inspection turns up something, we may renegotiate price or agree to repairs. I'll give you a straight read on what's reasonable to concede and what isn't.

Step 9

Fulfilling the Conditions

Once the buyer is satisfied, conditions get removed by way of a Notice of Fulfillment, a Waiver, or an Amendment, signed and delivered before the conditional period expires. Once you acknowledge receipt, the sale becomes firm.

I send all required documentation to your lawyer, who takes it from there through to closing, and I'll stay in touch with you the whole way for anything that comes up.

Step 10

Closing & Celebrate

The buyer typically does a final walkthrough shortly before closing to confirm the home's condition. Your lawyer handles the funds and paperwork on closing day, and once it's registered, the keys change hands. Congratulations, you've just sold your home.

Glossary

Terms worth knowing

Comparative Market Analysis (CMA)

A report comparing your home to recently sold, active, and expired listings to determine a realistic market value.

Days on market

How long a listing has been active, a key factor in how buyers and other agents perceive a property.

Exclusive listing

An agreement where one brokerage represents the seller and is entitled to commission if the home sells during the term, regardless of who finds the buyer.

Feature sheet

A one-page marketing document highlighting a property's key details and selling points.

Firm sale

An accepted offer where all conditions have been satisfied or waived, the deal is now binding.

Chattels vs. fixtures

Chattels are movable items (like furniture) not included unless stated. Fixtures are permanently attached (like light fixtures) and are included unless excluded.

Holdback

An amount held in trust after closing, often for outstanding repairs or unresolved items.

Listing agreement

The contract between you and your brokerage authorizing the sale of your property, including commission and term.

Multiple representation

When one brokerage represents both buyer and seller in the same transaction, subject to specific disclosure rules.

Net sheet

An estimate of what a seller will walk away with after commission, fees, and payouts.

Status certificate

For condo sales, a document confirming the unit's financial and legal standing within the corporation.

Right of first refusal

A clause giving a specific party the chance to match a competing offer before you accept it.

One More Thing

A few tips for sellers

  • Price it right from day one, overpricing costs more than underpricing.
  • Small fixes almost always beat big renovations dollar for dollar.
  • Professional photos aren't optional, most buyers decide online first.
  • Declutter and depersonalize before photos, not after the first showing feedback.
  • Know your numbers (commission, payout, closing costs) before you list, not after an offer lands.
  • Ask your REALTOR® before spending on any renovation.
  • Timing matters, London's market has real seasonal patterns.
  • Be flexible with showings, more access generally means more offers.
  • Don't take low offers personally, they're often just an opening move.
  • Leave the house during showings when you can.
  • Get a lawyer lined up early, usually a referral from family, friends, or your REALTOR®.
  • Choose a REALTOR® who'll tell you the truth, not just what you want to hear.
