01 · Positioning

Who this brand is

The foundation everything else in this guide gets built on.

Chase Raymond is a bold, modern real estate brand built inside Keller Williams' trusted red-and-white identity. It's built on confidence over clutter: strong red fields, an offset white panel, bold flush-left type. No stock templates, no generic sign layouts. The brand should feel like a local, personal, standout presence, not a franchise default.

04 · Signature Layout,"The Offset Block"

The one move that makes this brand recognizable

Every asset (sign, card, future social template) should use this same construction so it's instantly recognizable as yours before anyone reads a word.

1. FieldSolid KW Red fills the full canvas. No gradients, no photo backgrounds behind text.
2. Offset panelA white rectangle holds the headline, placed off-center (left-aligned, never centered), bleeding off one edge.
3. DividerA short white or red rule separates headline from name/contact block.
4. HierarchyEyebrow label → Name → Contact, always in that order, always left-aligned, generous white space around it.
05 · Logo Usage

KW logo + personal name lockup

The Keller Williams mark is licensed, not yours to alter,treat it as fixed. Your name lockup is the part you own.

Do
  • Use the official KW logo file provided by your office, untouched
  • Keep clear space around the KW mark equal to the height of the "k"
  • Pair "REALTOR®" (eyebrow style) directly above "CHASE RAYMOND" (headline style)
  • Place the KW logo top-left or top-right corner, consistently across assets
Don't
  • Recolor, rotate, stretch, or add effects to the KW logo
  • Place the KW logo smaller than legibility allows (min ~0.5in height in print)
  • Crowd the logo with other text or graphics inside its clear space
  • Create a new "Chase Raymond" logo mark that competes visually with KW's
